# Preliminary Database Design

This is a design baseline, not a Prisma schema. Exact PostgreSQL types, indexes, nullability, and migrations will be finalized in TASK-002.

## Conventions

- Internal primary keys: UUIDs generated by the database.
- Audit timestamps: `createdAt` and `updatedAt`; avoid hard deletion of records needed by booking history.
- Monetary values: integer minor units plus an ISO currency code.
- Appointment instants: timezone-aware `startAt` and `endAt`, modeled as a half-open interval `[startAt, endAt)`.
- Recurring rules: local weekday/time interpreted using `BusinessSettings.timezone`.
- Public identifiers: independent, high-entropy, non-sequential references; never expose an internal UUID as the only proof of access.

## Entities

### User

`id`, unique normalized `email`, `name`, `passwordHash`, `role` (`ADMIN` or `STAFF`), `isActive`, optional `lastLoginAt`, audit timestamps. A user represents authenticated personnel, not a customer account.

### Service

`id`, unique `slug`, `name`, `description`, `durationMinutes`, optional `bufferBeforeMinutes`/`bufferAfterMinutes`, `priceMinor`, `currency`, `isActive`, display order, audit timestamps. Bookings snapshot relevant service facts so later catalog edits do not rewrite history.

### StaffService

Join entity with `staffId` → `User`, `serviceId` → `Service`, optional staff-specific duration/price overrides, `isActive`, and audit timestamps. Unique on `(staffId, serviceId)`.

### AvailabilityRule

`id`, `staffId` → `User`, weekday, local `startTime`/`endTime`, optional effective date range, `isActive`, audit timestamps. Multiple windows per day are allowed. Business opening hours remain in business settings or a dedicated child table selected during schema design.

### BlockedTime

`id`, optional `staffId` → `User` (null may represent a studio-wide closure), `startAt`, `endAt`, category/reason, optional notes, `createdByUserId`, audit timestamps. The end must be after the start; overlap is allowed because multiple reasons can describe unavailable time.

### Booking

`id`, unique `publicReference`, `serviceId` → `Service`, `staffId` → `User`, `startAt`, `endAt`, `status`, customer name, normalized email and phone, optional customer notes, service name/duration/price/currency snapshots, `createdBy` channel, cancellation/reschedule metadata, optional `rescheduledFromBookingId`, and audit timestamps. `endAt` is persisted for queryability and constrained to be after `startAt`.

Historical bookings retain their original staff/service links where possible and their immutable snapshots even when a service or staff account becomes inactive. Cancellation should change state rather than delete the record. A reschedule may update the same booking with an audit event or create a linked replacement; that policy requires approval.

### BookingStatusEvent

`id`, `bookingId` → `Booking`, `fromStatus`, `toStatus`, actor user ID when authenticated, actor type/channel, reason code, optional safely limited note, and `createdAt`. Events are append-only and provide lifecycle auditability. Reschedule events should capture old/new time and staff values, either in structured metadata or a dedicated event type.

### BusinessSettings

Singleton initially: `id`, IANA `timezone`, currency, booking `slotIntervalMinutes`, `minimumLeadMinutes`, `bookingHorizonDays`, cancellation/reschedule cutoff values, default opening-hours representation, contact/display settings, audit timestamps. Validate that settings combinations are viable.

### RateLimitBucket

`id` or unique hashed key, action/scope, window start, request count, expiry, and audit timestamp. Keys derived from identifiers/IPs must be HMACed or otherwise pseudonymized. PostgreSQL is acceptable initially; a dedicated distributed store may replace it if traffic or serverless contention warrants.

## Relationships

- `User` many-to-many `Service` through `StaffService`.
- `User` one-to-many `AvailabilityRule`, `BlockedTime`, and assigned `Booking`.
- `Service` one-to-many `Booking`; bookings also preserve service snapshots.
- `Booking` one-to-many `BookingStatusEvent` and optional self-referential reschedule lineage.
- `BusinessSettings` supplies rules and timezone used across availability and booking use cases.

## Constraints and indexes

- Check positive durations/prices and `endAt > startAt`.
- Unique normalized user email, service slug, booking public reference, and staff-service pair.
- Index active services, bookings by `(staffId, startAt)`, bookings by `(status, startAt)`, blocks by `(staffId, startAt)`, and expiring rate-limit buckets.
- Prevent active booking overlap with a PostgreSQL exclusion constraint conceptually equivalent to: same `staffId` and intersecting `tstzrange(startAt, endAt, '[)')`, filtered to statuses that occupy the schedule (`PENDING`/`CONFIRMED`, subject to policy).

The exclusion constraint is the concurrency-safe final guard. Application checks improve messages and avoid unnecessary writes, but they cannot replace it. Creation and rescheduling must use a transaction and translate constraint violations into a safe “slot no longer available” response.
